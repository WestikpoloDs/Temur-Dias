Ada Lovelace

About Ada Lovelace

The first computer programmer in history

Ada Lovelace (1815–1852) was an English mathematician widely regarded as the author of the world's first computer program. She worked with Charles Babbage on his Analytical Engine and was the first to describe how the machine could carry out complex calculations following a set of instructions. Her notes on a translation of an article about the Analytical Engine effectively became the first published description of programming.

Key Events

  1. 1815 — Born in London, daughter of poet Lord Byron
  2. 1833 — Meets Charles Babbage
  3. 1843 — Publishes her notes on the Analytical Engine
  4. 1843 — Describes the first algorithm intended for a machine
  5. 1852 — Passes away at the age of 36

Contributions to Science

  • The first algorithm designed to be carried out by a machine
  • The idea that machines could process more than just numbers
  • The concept of loops and repeated operations in computing
  • Inspiration for the Ada programming language

Ada Lovelace's work was decades ahead of its time — real computing machines didn't appear until about a hundred years after her death. Today the Ada programming language is named in her honor, and the second Tuesday of October is celebrated as Ada Lovelace Day.
